# BEACON Phone Module: independent schematic review

Reviewer stance: a skeptical senior EE. The goal is to find what will not work on the first build.
Scope: the KiCad 10 schematic generated by `scripts/design.py` and `scripts/parts.py`, the netlist exported
on 2026-11-02 (`kicad-cli sch export netlist`), the kicad-happy `analyze_schematic.py` run, and a
side-by-side comparison with the Altium PDF `BeaconPhoneModule.pdf` (9 sheets).

Reference designators are taken from the 2026-11-02 03:34 netlist. If the generator renumbers them,
use the net names, which are stable.

Not re-reported because they are already known open items: LT80602 pin numbers and footprint (the only
ERC warning), and the placeholder loop-filter values (R29-R31, C64-C67).

ERC: 0 errors, 1 warning (the LT80602 footprint is missing from `BEACON.pretty`, a known item).

## RF level plan

Assumptions: TX conducted power +20 dBm at J4. FL1 (2450BP08) insertion loss about 2 dB. The QPL9547 at
2.45 GHz and 5 V/65 mA has gain of about 16.5 dB (interpolated from the 1.9/2.6 GHz table), OP1dB of
about +23 dBm, and therefore IP1dB of about +6.5 dBm. Its RF input absolute maximum is +22 dBm. The nRF
matching network loses about 0.5 dB.

nRF52840 limits from PS v1.11:
- PRX,MAX is 0 dBm (maximum received level at <0.1 % PER).
- The RF input absolute maximum is +10 dBm.
- BLE 1M C/I is roughly -40 to -48 dB at offsets of 3 MHz or more.

### TX chain

| Node | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LMX2582 RFoutAP (single-ended, 50 ohm pull-up) | up to about +8 dBm (DS: 8 dBm typ at 3 GHz) | Set with OUTA_PWR. OUTAM is terminated in 50 ohm (C70/R34). |
| CW_RF, after C69 | about +8 dBm max | |
| PA_IN, after the 3 dB pad R20/R21/R22 | about +5 dBm max | The pad values (294/17.8/294) are a correct 3.0 dB 50 ohm pi. |
| CC2595 output | about +20.7 dBm at 0 dBm in. TI quotes +22.5 dBm at 3.3 V with +3 dBm in. | PA input absolute maximum is +10 dBm, so there is 5 dB margin even at full LMX output. |
| J4, after the match, C42 and the LPF | about +20 dBm | |

PA drive is adequate and safe. The real limit is firmware: OUTA_PWR must cap PA_IN at about 0 dBm.
Without that cap the PA reaches about +22.5 dBm, which matters for the regulatory limit (finding S1).

### RX chain versus carrier leakage from TX antenna J4 into RX antenna J3

| Antenna isolation | J3 | LNA_IN | LNA_OUT | nRF ANT | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 dB | +5 | +3 | about +19 (compressed) | about **+18.5 dBm** | Exceeds the nRF +10 dBm absolute maximum. **Damage risk.** |
| 20 dB | 0 | -2 | +14.5 | **+14 dBm** | Exceeds the absolute maximum. |
| 25 dB | -5 | -7 | +9.5 | +9 dBm | At the absolute maximum. Receiver saturated. |
| 30 dB | -10 | -12 | +4.5 | +4 dBm | Above PRX,MAX. Receiver blocked. |
| 35 dB | -15 | -17 | -0.5 | -1 dBm | Barely usable. |
| 45 dB | -25 | -27 | -10.5 | -11 dBm | Usable: the backscatter must be at least about -70 dBm at J3. |

Because the carrier and the backscatter pass through the same gain, range is set by the ratio between
them, not by noise figure:

    S_J3(min) ~ +20 - Isolation - 45 dB

The +45 dB is the nRF C/I at 3 MHz or more offset. Assuming 1 m TX to tag and 1 m tag to RX
(40 dB free-space path loss each way), 2 dBi antennas and 6-10 dB tag conversion loss, the backscatter
at J3 is about -66 to -70 dBm. **At 1 m that needs about 45 dB of TX-to-RX isolation.** Two U.FL
antennas on a phone dongle typically give 10-25 dB.

## BLOCKERS

### B1. The nRF52840 ANT pin is overdriven whenever the PA runs: carrier leaks through J4 to J3, then FL1, U6 and U5.H23
- **Nets:** TX_ANT (J4), RX_ANT (J3), RX_BPF, LNA_IN, LNA_OUT, RX_RF, NRF_ANT.
- **Problem:** FL1 (2450BP08) passes the whole ISM band, so the in-band CW carrier is not attenuated.
  The QPL9547 then adds about 16.5 dB. With realistic dongle isolation of 15-25 dB, the nRF ANT sees
  +9 to +18 dBm (see the table above). That is above the nRF52840 RF-input absolute maximum (+10 dBm)
  and far above PRX,MAX (0 dBm). At best the receiver is fully blocked; at worst the nRF is damaged.
  The LNA also compresses (IP1dB about +6.5 dBm) at 15 dB isolation. Shutting the LNA down with
  LNA_PD is no help: it only removes about 20 dB of gain (S21 of about -3.5 dB).
- **Fix (schematic):**
  1. Make the LNA optional. Add a 0 ohm bypass footprint from RX_BPF to RX_RF, DNP by default, plus
     0 ohm DNP links in series with C31 and C32, so the board can be built with or without U6.
  2. Add a pi-pad footprint (3 x 0402) between C32 and RX_RF, fitted as 0 ohm series with DNP shunts,
     so 6-20 dB of attenuation can be added on the bench.
  3. Add a footprint for a low-capacitance RF limiter or ESD diode at NRF_ANT (see S4).
- **Fix (system):** specify and measure J4-to-J3 isolation. You need at least 30 dB to keep the nRF
  under its absolute maximum with the LNA fitted, and about 45 dB for roughly 1 m range. Use separation
  and orthogonal polarization, put the ground plane between the antennas, or use a patch for RX (the
  Altium drawing calls it RX_PATCH). Run the tag subcarrier offset as large as possible, 20 MHz or more,
  with the carrier at one band edge. Reduce TX power (OUTA_PWR) when the tag is close.
- **Firmware interlock:** never set PA_EN high with LNA_PD low and the nRF RX active until isolation has
  been measured.

## SHOULD-FIX

### S1. A +20 dBm unmodulated CW carrier is not a legal operating mode under FCC 15.247
- **Nets/parts:** U8, U7, TX_ANT.
- **Problem:** 15.247 covers DTS (6 dB bandwidth of at least 500 kHz) or FHSS. A fixed CW carrier
  falls under 15.249, which allows only 50 mV/m at 3 m (about -1 dBm EIRP). The CC2595 can reach
  +22.5 dBm when the LMX drive is high.
- **Fix:** plan to frequency-hop the carrier with the LMX2582 over SPI. 15.247(b)(1) allows 0.125 W
  (+21 dBm) for 2.4 GHz FHSS with fewer than 75 channels and at least 15 channels at 0.4 s dwell. That
  makes loop bandwidth and VCO-calibration time a design input for the loop filter (still a placeholder).
  Limit PA_IN to 0 dBm or less in firmware. For the capstone demo, run under an experimental licence or
  in a shielded test setup.

### S2. Restricted-band harmonics: 2nd harmonic 4.80-4.97 GHz and part of the 3rd at 7.2-7.45 GHz
- **Parts:** U7 (saturated class-AB PA), FL2 2450LP07, L7/C41.
- **Problem:** the restricted bands at 4.5-5.15 GHz and 7.25-7.75 GHz allow about -41 dBm EIRP. At
  +20 dBm conducted, the 2nd harmonic must be -61 dBc or lower. A saturated CC2595 gives about -20 to
  -30 dBc, and one LTCC LPF adds about 30 dB at 2fo (verify against the Johanson curve). The margin is
  zero to negative. The VCO also runs at exactly 2fo (4.8 GHz, CHDIV=2), so the PA's 2nd harmonic can
  injection-pull the VCO.
- **Fix:** add a DNP shunt-C / series-L / shunt-C pi footprint between FL2 and J4 (or a second LPF
  footprint) for tuning. Plan a shield-can footprint over U8 and over U7/FL2, and keep the PA output far
  from the VCO pins.

### S3. Power budget is within 500 mA but with little margin, and the LNA is ON at boot (R19 pull-down)
- **Budget:**

  | Load | Current |
  |---|---|
  | LMX2582 | 250 mA typ (DS 6.5) |
  | CC2595 | about 98 mA at +20.7 dBm (TI), more at +22 dBm |
  | nRF52840 RX + USB + CPU | about 15 mA |
  | TCXO, INA219 x2, LED | about 6 mA |
  | **3V3 total** | **about 370-410 mA (1.25-1.35 W)** |

  The buck runs at about 88 % efficiency, so the 3V3 rail draws about 0.30-0.32 A from 5V0. Adding
  the QPL9547 at 65 mA typ (100 mA max) gives **0.37-0.42 A typ and about 0.48 A worst case** at the USB
  plug.
- **Problems:**
  - USB 2.0 allows 100 mA before configuration, and some phones cap OTG near 500 mA (iPhone at 4.5 W).
  - R19 (100k LNA_SD to GND) turns the 65 mA LNA on as soon as 5V0 appears, before firmware runs.
- **Fix:**
  - Swap R19 to a 100k pull-up from LNA_SD to 3V3, so the LNA is off by default. Shut Down is active
    high and needs more than 1.17 V. Note that it still draws 4.3 mA in shutdown.
  - Have firmware enable the synth, PA and LNA only after USB configuration.
  - Optionally raise R16 to cut LNA bias. Linearity hardly matters here, because the carrier, not noise,
    sets the range.

### S4. No ESD protection on either antenna port (J3 RX_ANT, J4 TX_ANT)
- **Problem:** RX ESD passes through FL1 and C31 straight into the QPL9547 input and then into the nRF
  ANT. TX ESD reaches the CC2595 open-drain output through FL2 and C42. Both antennas are external or
  user-connectable.
- **Fix:**
  - Add a 0402 shunt inductor footprint (about 22-27 nH, self-resonant frequency well above 2.5 GHz)
    from RX_ANT to GND and from TX_ANT to GND. This gives a DC/ESD discharge path that is about +j400 ohm
    at 2.45 GHz.
  - And/or add an ultra-low-capacitance RF TVS (0.1-0.2 pF class, for example Infineon ESD0P2RF or ST
    ESDARF02) at each U.FL.
  - On the TX side, check that the diode handles +20 dBm without clipping (the working peak voltage at
    +20 dBm into 50 ohm is about 3.2 V).

### S5. The 5V0 and VCC_SYN margins are thin, and the VBUS bulk capacitance is above 10 uF
- **VCC_SYN margin:** the setpoint is 0.8 x (1 + 681k/220k) = 3.276 V, assuming the 0.8 V reference in
  the notes (verify against the LT80602 datasheet). The 3V3 shunt R5 (about 20 mV) and FB2 (about 15 mV)
  sit outside the regulation loop. With ±1.5 % reference and divider tolerance, VCC_SYN can sit at about
  3.19 V against the LMX2582 minimum of 3.15 V.
  **Fix:** change R9 from 681k to 698k (1 %), which gives 3.338 V nominal. That is still under the
  limits of the nRF (3.6 V), the CC2595 (3.6 V) and the LMX (3.45 V).
- **VBUS bulk capacitance:** C1 + C21 + C6 + C7 + C8 + C33 + C34 + C35 is about 17 uF directly on
  VBUS. The buck soft-start only limits the 3V3-side capacitance. The polyfuse and shunt resistance is
  only about 0.15-0.35 ohm, so the plug-in inrush peak is several amps. Many phones trip their OTG
  over-current protection on this and report "accessory not supported".
  **Fix:** either add a slew-rate-limited load switch between 5V_BUS and 5V0 (for example TPS22918 with
  CT set for a 1-2 ms ramp), or cut C6 to 4.7 uF and keep the total under 10 uF.

### S6. LNA supply (LNA_VCC) is hard-wired with R17 0 ohm to the buck input node 5V0
- **Problem:** 5V0 carries the LT80602 pulsed input current. Any ripple on LNA_VCC AM-modulates the
  large leaked carrier going through the LNA. That creates sidebands at carrier ± fsw (about 1 MHz with
  R8 = 37.4k) and harmonics, which can land on the backscatter channel.
- **Fix:** replace R17 with a ferrite bead (BLM18PG221SN1, as already used) and add 4.7-10 uF at
  LNA_VCC. Better still, feed the LNA from a small 3.3 V or 4.5 V LDO.

### S7. Phase noise and reciprocal mixing from the leaked carrier
- **Problem:** at a 10 MHz offset, LMX2582 open-loop noise at 2.4 GHz (5.5 GHz -151 dBc/Hz, less 6 dB
  for divide-by-2) is about -157 dBc/Hz, or about -97 dBc in a 1 MHz BLE channel. With 20 dB isolation
  the carrier at J3 is 0 dBm, so the in-channel noise is about -97 dBm, at the nRF sensitivity level.
  The CC2595 adds its own wideband noise floor on top.
- **Fix:** this is another argument for 40 dB or more of isolation (B1). Also choose the loop bandwidth
  (placeholder now) so that the closed-loop noise at the tag offset is set by the VCO, not by PLL
  in-band noise. Avoid tag offsets near PFD multiples (52 MHz) and near 26 MHz or 32 MHz reference
  harmonics in band: 2392, 2418, 2444, 2470, 2400, 2432 and 2464 MHz.

## NICE-TO-HAVE

1. **LT80602 SYNC/MODE = GND.** Check the datasheet. In most ADI Silent Switcher parts, GND selects
   Burst Mode. For an RF board, pulse-skip or forced-continuous mode (fixed frequency, no
   low-frequency burst ripple on 3V3) is preferable. Fit a 0 ohm option to GND, INTVCC or float.
   During CW operation the load is above 0.3 A, so the part is in CCM anyway.
2. **BST capacitor C11 is 1 uF.** Most Silent Switcher parts specify 0.1 uF, so check the LT80602
   datasheet. Note that the Altium drawing used 22 uF on the buck input while KiCad has 10 uF (C6).
   That change is undocumented in the notes, so either document it as an inrush decision (S5) or restore it.
3. **The three 33 ohm resistors on the Altium MCU sheet were dropped** (page 5, unconnected R? 33 x3).
   They were almost certainly meant as series terminations on SYN_SCK, SYN_SDI and SYN_CSB. Add them
   near U5 to slow SPI edges next to a 4.8 GHz VCO.
4. **DEC5 (N24) is open.** PS Fig. 221 says 820 pF is "not required for Fxx and later" build codes. Add
   a DNP 820 pF footprint in case older-build-code stock is fitted.
5. **PA / synth interlock.** Firmware must confirm lock (MUXout configured as lock detect) before
   setting PA_EN. Optionally gate PA_EN in hardware with SYN_MUXOUT through a single AND gate
   (74LVC1G08) so the PA cannot radiate an unlocked or default-frequency LMX2582. Boot state is
   otherwise safe: PA_EN has a 100k pull-down (R26) and SYN_CE has a 100k pull-down (R14).
6. **SYN_MUXOUT floats** when the LMX is powered down. Enable the nRF internal pull-down, or add 100k.
7. **CC2595 power control.** On the EVM, RBIAS1/2 go through 20k to a separate VPC/VPC2 control node
   with 0.1 uF (C14/C85). Here R24/R25 go straight to VDD_PA. Add a 0 ohm option so RBIAS can be fed
   from an RC-filtered nRF PWM, which gives a hardware TX-power trim for S1 and S2. This needs a check
   against SWRS090.
8. **Ferrite bead plus ceramic resonance.** FB1 with C36 (VDD_PA) and FB2 with C43 plus three 10 uF
   (VCC_SYN) form high-Q LC tanks in the tens of kHz. Add about 1 ohm in series with one bulk cap, or
   use a tantalum or polymer cap, to damp them.
9. **nRF VBUS absolute maximum is 5.8 V,** while the D1 SMF6.0A breakdown is at least 6.67 V. A
   misbehaving 6 V source would pass through. This is low risk from a phone and acceptable as is.
10. **Thermal.** Total dissipation is about 1.6 W: LMX 0.83 W, LNA 0.33 W, CC2595 about 0.2 W, buck
    0.17 W, plus the rest, all in a dongle that is touching a phone. Expect a 30-40 °C rise in a small
    plastic enclosure. Duty-cycle the synth and PA, and give U8 and U7 full thermal-via arrays to an
    inner ground plane.
11. **Test access.** Add test points on 5V0, 3V3_OUT, VCC_SYN, VDD_PA and LNA_VCC. Add a 0 ohm series
    link plus U.FL or coax-probe pad on PA_IN and on TX_LPF so LMX output and PA output can be measured
    separately.
12. **Ground symbols.** The Altium USB sheet put SH on chassis ground and R1 (CC Rd) on a separate
    ground symbol. KiCad merges everything into GND. That is fine for a plug into a phone, but state it
    as a decision.
13. **nRF TX must never be enabled.** The nRF ANT drives into the LNA output, so firmware should set
    TXPOWER to its minimum and never start TX. This includes BLE advertising.
14. **Coexistence with the phone's own radios.** FL1 gives little rejection at the edges of LTE B40
    (2.30-2.40 GHz) and B41 (2.50-2.69 GHz). The phone's Wi-Fi/BT is in band and centimetres away. A
    BAW coexistence filter in place of the LTCC filter would reduce LNA compression from the host phone.

## Checked and OK (no action)

- **I2C:** 4.7k pull-ups R12/R13 to 3V3, two devices at 0x40 and 0x41, no conflict.
- **Pull-ups:** PG pull-up R11 is present. BUTTON uses the internal pull-up. nRESET uses the internal
  pull-up (enable it in UICR PSELRESET).
- **nRF radio-sensitive pins:** only static or slow signals use them: PA_EN on P0.02, 3V3_PG on P0.03,
  SPARE_IO1 on P0.28, LED on P1.06 (0.9 mA, standard drive). The SPI to the synth (P0.19-P0.23), I2C
  (P0.13/P0.15), UART (P0.06/P0.08) and SWO (P1.00) all avoid them. Keep SPARE_IO1 (P0.28) at 10 kHz or
  below in firmware.
- **Clocks:** 32 MHz crystal with 12 pF caps is correct for CL = 8 pF. Running without a 32.768 kHz
  crystal (LFRC) is acceptable.
- **LMX2582 OSCin:** AC coupling from the TCXO, OSCinM terminated in 50 ohm, R27 DNP. Clipped-sine TCXO
  levels (0.8 Vpp or more) exceed REFv min (0.2 Vppd).
- **Shunts:** dissipation at 0.45 A is about 10 mW in a 0805. The 10 ohm/100 nF Kelvin filters are
  within TI guidance. IN+ bias current x 10 ohm gives about 0.2 mV, or about 4 mA of error.
- **F1 NANOSMDC075F-2:** 0.75 A hold and 1.5 A trip against a 0.42 A typical load. It derates to about
  0.6 A at 50 °C, which still passes.
- **D1 TVS:** cathode on 5V_BUS (KiCad D pin 1 = K), correct.

## Top 10 (one line each)

1. BLOCKER B1: carrier leaking J4 to J3 is amplified by U6, so NRF_ANT sees +9 to +18 dBm at 15-25 dB isolation, above the nRF +10 dBm absolute maximum. Add LNA bypass/pad footprints and require 30 dB isolation minimum (45 dB for about 1 m range).
2. S1: +20 dBm fixed CW is not 15.247-compliant. Plan LMX2582 hopping (15 channels or more, +21 dBm max) and cap PA_IN at 0 dBm or less (the CC2595 reaches +22.5 dBm at +3 dBm in).
3. S2: 2fo and 3fo fall in restricted bands and need -61 dBc or better; CC2595 plus one LPF is marginal. Add a DNP pi footprint after FL2 and shield cans. VCO = 2fo means pulling risk.
4. S3: LNA_SD pull-down R19 turns on the 65 mA LNA at boot. Make it a pull-up to 3V3. The budget is 0.37-0.42 A typ / about 0.48 A worst of 500 mA.
5. S4: no ESD on RX_ANT/TX_ANT. Add shunt 22-27 nH and/or 0.2 pF RF TVS footprints at J3 and J4.
6. S5a: VCC_SYN can sag to about 3.19 V against the LMX minimum of 3.15 V. Change R9 from 681k to 698k (3.34 V).
7. S5b: about 17 uF directly on VBUS. Add a slew-controlled load switch or cut C6 so phone OTG over-current protection does not trip on plug-in.
8. S6: LNA_VCC hard-tied (R17 0 ohm) to the buck input node. Use a ferrite plus 10 uF or an LDO to stop AM sidebands at carrier ± fsw.
9. Nice-to-have: restore the Altium 33 ohm series resistors on SYN_SCK/SDI/CSB, and document or restore the 22 uF buck CIN (now 10 uF C6).
10. Nice-to-have: add a hardware PA_EN AND lock-detect interlock, and confirm SYNC/MODE (GND = Burst Mode?) and the 1 uF BST cap against the LT80602 datasheet.
